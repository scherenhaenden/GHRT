# 04 — Universal Coupling to the Trace

Definitions in the Einstein frame: $T_E = g^{\mu\nu} T^E_{\mu\nu}$; $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\chi)g_{\mu\nu}$; $\alpha = d\ln A / d\chi$; $\beta = M_{\rm Pl}\alpha$. The scalar equation schema is $\Box\chi = U_{,\chi} - \alpha T_E$.

With signature $(-,+,+,+)$, for a perfect fluid $T = -\rho + 3p$ ($\rho$ is energy density in natural units). For ideal radiation, $p = \rho/3$, hence $T = 0$. For dust, $p \approx 0$, $T \approx -\rho$. For an isotropic monoenergetic population of massive particles, $|T|/\rho = \gamma^{-2}$. This relation must not be generalized without integrating the velocity and pressure distribution.

A radiation-dominated environment does not automatically generate trace screening: quantum trace/thermal anomalies, nonrelativistic particles, and temperature corrections require separate treatment. Nor does it mean that electromagnetic waves “do not gravitate”: their tensor has zero trace but a nonzero stress-energy tensor.

For dust, define $\rho_{\ast} = \rho_E / A = A^3 \widetilde\rho$ and $U_{\rm eff} = U + \rho_{\ast}[A - 1]$. If $A = \exp(\chi^2 / (2M^2))$, in the regime $\chi^2/M^2 \ll 1$:

$$
U_{\rm eff} \approx U + \rho_{\ast}\frac{\chi^2}{2M^2}.
$$

The symbols $\rho_m$ in earlier notes must be interpreted as the appropriate nonrelativistic auxiliary density, with declared units and frames; do not replace it with total radiation density.

A universal coupling in the Jordan frame helps formulate the weak equivalence principle for matter, but does not guarantee the absence of all observable violations in other sectors.
